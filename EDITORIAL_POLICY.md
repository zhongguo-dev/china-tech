# Editorial and inclusion policy

## Purpose

China Tech is a public, curated reference for technologies and organizations relevant to software developers and researchers who want to understand China's developer ecosystem. It complements the [Zhongguo.dev Directory](https://www.zhongguo.dev/directory) and follows its technical taxonomy.

The GitHub index reflects the eligible, published Directory, not a ranked collection of favorites.

## Inclusion criteria

An entry should meet all of these standards:

1. **Developer relevance.** It is a project, tool, framework, model, infrastructure technology, developer-oriented product or platform, research effort, or organization with a clear connection to software or technical work.
2. **Supported connection to China.** Its origin or meaningful development within China's technology ecosystem is supported by credible evidence. A Chinese name, Chinese-language README, or individual contributor's background is not proof by itself.
3. **Identifiable subject.** Its identity can be distinguished from similarly named projects, versions, products, organizations, and forks.
4. **Reliable references.** At least one appropriate primary source supports the entity's identity and description, with other sources used when necessary.
5. **Accurate description.** Descriptions are concise, neutral, technically useful, and avoid unsupported metrics or claims.

Open-source licensing is **not** a prerequisite. Do not label proprietary software, source-available work, or open-weight models as open source without license evidence. A public GitHub repository is not required.

## Organization and classification

The catalog uses the four technical areas and twelve topics published on Zhongguo.dev. Topic placement follows the available editorial evidence. An entry may be relevant to multiple topics, but it keeps one canonical identity and profile link. Where a topic is uncertain, leave it unassigned rather than guessing.

Entries may include the official name, a brief English description, supported Chinese name, official website, repository or documentation link, topic, and Zhongguo.dev profile. Omit unknown factual fields rather than infer them.

## Quality and correction standards

- Prefer official documentation, maintained project repositories, original announcements, research publications, and first-party organization pages.
- Attribute factual claims to the appropriate sources.
- Do not fabricate stars, benchmarks, activity, adoption, company information, licenses, or verification.
- Distinguish software projects from companies, model families, individual releases, and organizations.
- Review duplicates, stale references, name changes, and reported inaccuracies.
- Record necessary corrections or removals through the normal editorial review process.

## What inclusion means

Inclusion helps discovery. It is **not** an endorsement, security audit, certification, recommendation, or guarantee of maintenance or production readiness. Listing order does not imply relative merit or ranking.

## Suggestions and decisions

The public can submit suggestions and corrections through GitHub Issues. Maintainers assess submissions using the standards above. Decisions may include acceptance, revision, deferral, duplicate, or decline. Publication follows review; it is never automatic from a public submission.

## Attribution

Third-party names, artwork, repositories, papers, and linked materials remain under their respective licenses and rights. Published summaries are original editorial text wherever possible. Commercial relationships, if any, must not be presented as independent editorial endorsements.
